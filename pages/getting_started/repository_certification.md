Research data repositories use certification to demonstrate that they meet recognised standards for data quality, security, preservation, and accessibility. It promotes transparency and accountability by providing an independent assessment of a repository's policies, processes, and infrastructure.

## What is repository certification?

Researchers, data stewards, funders and other stakeholders need a framework of internationally recognised certification standards for digital repositories, in order to assess and improve the quality of their work processes and management systems. A term often used in this respect is [Trustworthy Digital Repository](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-28032-0_42) (TDR): a repository that ensures long-term preservation, accessibility, and reuse of research outputs. In the European Framework for Audit and Certification of Digital Repositories, three kinds of certification with increasing degrees of complexity and depth are available:

- [CoreTrustSeal](https://www.coretrustseal.org/) (CTS): based on the [Data Seal of Approval](https://phys-techsciences.datastations.nl/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.17026/dans-28z-njxq) (DSA) and the [World Data System](https://worlddatasystem.org/) (WDS), this is the most widely adopted certification, focusing on core requirements such as data integrity, accessibility, and sustainability. Certified repositories are [listed by CTS](https://amt.coretrustseal.org/certificates).
- [nestor Seal](https://www.langzeitarchivierung.de/Webs/nestor/EN/Home/home_node.html): verification according to DIN 31644.
- [ISO 16363](https://www.iso.org/standard/87472.html): formal certification.

The assessments vary in intensity, from a peer review of completed documentation (self-assessment) to a prepared on-site visit by an external audit team. These instruments are used worldwide. Data sponsors, producers and re-users may trust any managing body that has been certified according to one of these standards.

## Why is repository certification important?

[CoreTrustSeal](http://www.coretrustseal.org/why-certification/) puts it this way: "If we want to be able to share data, we need to store them in a trustworthy data repository. Data created and used by scientists should be managed, curated, and archived in such a way to preserve the initial investment in collecting them. Researchers must be certain that data held in archives remain useful and meaningful into the future. Funding authorities increasingly require continued access to data produced by the projects they fund, and have made this an important element in Data Management Plans. Indeed, some funders now stipulate that the data they fund must be deposited in a trustworthy repository."
