### What is Repository Certification?
Researchers, data stewards, funders and other stakeholders need a framework of internationally recognised certification standards for digital repositories in order to assess and improve the quality of their work processes and management systems. A term often used in this respect is Trustworthy Digital Repository (TDR) which is  referring to repositories that ensure long-term preservation, accessibility, and reuse of research outputs. In the European Framework for Audit and Certification of Digital Repositories three kinds of certification with increasing degrees of complexity and depth are available:

- CoreTrustSeal (CTS): this is based on Data Seal of Approval (DSA) and World Data System (WDS) and represents the most widely adopted certification, focusing on core requirements such as data integrity, accessibility, and sustainability. Certified digital repositories are listed at CTS. 
- Nestor Seal: verification according to DIN 31644 
- ISO 16363 certification: formal level certification

The assessments vary in intensity from a peer review of completed documentation (self-assessment) to a prepared on-site visit by an external audit team. These instruments are used worldwide. Data sponsors, producers and re-users may trust any managing body that has been certified according to one of the above standards.



### Why is Repository certification important?
CTS: "... If we want to be able to share data, we need to store them in a trustworthy data repository. Data created and used by scientists should be managed, curated, and archived in such a way to preserve the initial investment in collecting them. Researchers must be certain that data held in archives remain useful and meaningful into the future. Funding authorities increasingly require continued access to data produced by the projects they fund, and have made this an important element in Data Management Plans. Indeed, some funders now stipulate that the data they fund must be deposited in a trustworthy repository. ..."
